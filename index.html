(Wei Jie) Reiner Ng, Ph.D.

Senior Member of Technical Staff, DSO National Laboratories

I graduated with a Ph.D. in Social Psychology (Nov 2024) from the National University of Singapore. My research examines attitudes—particularly attitudinal strength—and how Large Language Models (LLMs) can clarify how these cognitive properties inform downstream behaviors in humans and agents over time. I combine experimental design, cross-sectional surveys, and social media data with network analyses and multilevel meta-analyses in R.

Selected Publications

2024 · Journal of Communication (IF: 7.10) 133 samples · 22 countries

The influence of affective and cognitive appeals on persuasion outcomes: A cross-cultural meta-analysis

Wei Jie Reiner Ng, Ya Hui Michelle See, Mike W.-L. Cheung

A cross-cultural meta-analytic synthesis assessing how cultural individualism–collectivism moderates the efficacy of affect- versus cognition-based persuasive messaging.

Key finding: Using 133 samples across 22 countries (N = 29,338), we found affective appeals to be relatively more effective than cognitive appeals in collectivistic societies, whereas both appeals were similarly effective in individualistic societies.

2022 · Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin (IF: 4.38) 5 studies

When objective ambivalence leads to subjective ambivalence: An affect-cognition matching perspective

Wei Jie Reiner Ng, Ya Hui Michelle See, Laura E. Wallace

Investigates when evaluative inconsistency between positive and negative attributes translates into introspective, experienced subjective ambivalence.

Key finding: Across five studies, greater objective–subjective ambivalence correspondence occurred with an affective–cognitive match between message and topic orientation—primarily when the message was counterattitudinal.

2022 · Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin 4 studies

Defensive confidence and certainty in unchanged attitudes: The role of affect-cognition matching

Wei Jie Reiner Ng, Chi Bu, Ya Hui Michelle See

Examines how matching persuasive appeals to underlying affective vs. cognitive attitude bases affects defensive confidence and attitude certainty after resisting persuasion.

Key finding: Unlike a matched attack, an attack mismatched to the affective–cognitive orientation of attitudes results in low defensive confidence individuals holding onto unchanged attitudes with less certainty than high defensive confidence individuals, mediated by perceived knowledge.
